Phishing Kits Impersonating Mobile Banking Apps — Indicators & Detections

Adversary-in-the-middle kits relay customer credentials and one-time passwords in real time, letting fraudsters log in and move money within minutes. Here is how they work and how to detect them.

Key takeaways

  • Phishing kits now proxy the real banking site, capturing passwords and OTPs as the customer types them.
  • The fraudulent login typically comes from a new device within seconds of the OTP being issued — a strong detection signal.
  • Combining look-alike domain monitoring with login-behavior analytics gives your team the warning it needs before money moves.

01What we observed

Over recent weeks, Prenes.ai analysts have tracked phishing campaigns that impersonate the mobile and internet banking portals of retail banks and e-wallets. Victims are lured through SMS messages, messaging-app groups and paid social-media ads promising refunds, loyalty points or urgent account verification.

Unlike older phishing pages that simply collected passwords, these kits operate as an adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM): they forward every keystroke to the genuine bank in real time and pass the bank's responses back to the victim. The customer sees a working login flow — and the attacker gets a valid session.

02How the kit works

The kits we analyzed share a common design, sold or shared as a service among fraud groups:

  • Reverse-proxy core — the page loads content from the genuine banking site, so branding and flows always look current.
  • Real-time OTP relay — once the victim submits the OTP, the operator is notified instantly through a messaging bot and logs in from their own device.
  • Anti-analysis filters — pages only render for mobile user-agents and local IP ranges; security scanners and desktop browsers receive a harmless page.
  • Rapid domain rotation — each domain lives for hours to days, combining the bank's name with words such as "verify", "refund" or "support".
!
Why MFA alone is not enough. SMS and app-based OTPs are captured and replayed within their validity window. Phishing-resistant methods and transaction-level confirmation are needed to break the chain.

05Detection opportunities

Before the attack: domain monitoring

Monitor certificate-transparency logs and new domain registrations for strings combining your brand with high-risk keywords. Early takedown removes the page before the SMS campaign reaches customers.

During the attack: login behavior

The strongest signal is timing. In AiTM fraud, an OTP is requested by one session and used from a different device, IP range or ASN seconds later, often followed immediately by a device registration or new beneficiary.

Pseudo-rule · SIEM
// AiTM login: OTP issued and consumed from different networks
FROM auth_events
WHERE event_type IN ("otp_issued", "otp_verified")
GROUP BY user_id, otp_id
HAVING asn(otp_issued) != asn(otp_verified)
   AND time_between(otp_issued, otp_verified) < 120s
   AND device_is_new(otp_verified) = true
THEN alert(severity="P2", playbook="ato-mobile-banking")
i
Escalate to P1 when the same session adds a beneficiary or raises transfer limits within 10 minutes of login.

06Recommendations

Tick off items as your team completes them.

Security & fraud teams

  • Enable look-alike domain and certificate-transparency monitoringInclude Vietnamese keywords such as "xacthuc", "hoantien", "hotro".
  • Alert on OTP verification from a different ASN than the requesting session
  • Add a cooling-off period for new devices and new beneficiariesDelay high-value transfers from a device registered in the last 24 hours.
  • Pre-agree takedown procedures with registrars and hosting providers

Customer communication

  • Remind customers the bank never sends login links by SMS
  • Show device name and location in every OTP message
